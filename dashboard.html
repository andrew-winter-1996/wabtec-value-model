Methodology

This model translates AI-enabled obsolescence management into three layers of value: revenue Wabtec captures from incremental bookings (Layer 1), margin Wabtec captures from service delivery efficiency (Layer 2), and savings the customer captures from reduced maintenance spend (Layer 3, the demand driver). The revenue and COGS baseline are anchored to the Wabtec 2025 Annual Report. Uplift and efficiency ranges allow stress-testing across Bull, Base, and Bear scenarios.

CEO on Q1 2026 earnings call (Apr 22, 2026): "I'll probably start with obsolescence because that can drive, I think, some upside for us in terms of the opportunity to continue to modernize subsystems for our customers." — Rafael Santana, President & CEO, Wabtec
Layer 1: Revenue Uplift
Freight services base ($B/yr) Default: $3.4B · Fixed baseline
Source:
Wabtec 2025 Annual Report. Full-year freight services revenue used as the stable baseline for this model, reflecting the pre-EVO-ramp run-rate before Q1 2026 modernization delivery timing impacts.
Key context:
The EVO Modernization Program represents approximately 10,000 eligible units in Wabtec's ~24,600 locomotive installed base. As EVO commercialization ramps, the $3.4B baseline represents the floor from which bookings uplift is applied.
Range rationale:
Held fixed across scenarios. The scenario variable is the bookings uplift %, which reflects different assumptions about how quickly AI-enabled obsolescence management converts the pipeline.
Bookings uplift (%) Default: 15% · Range: 5 - 25%
Source:
Water Services OEM Demeter platform deployment (McKinsey internal), referenced in 20260327 Wabtec-McK Commercial Acceleration_vS.pdf. CEO commentary from Q1 2026 earnings call (Apr 22, 2026).
Benchmark:
Water OEM achieved 12-15% bookings uplift from AI-driven lead translation. Default raised to 15% following Wabtec CEO Rafael Santana's direct identification of obsolescence as a near-term upside driver (Q1 2026 call).
What drives the uplift:
The uplift flows primarily through Services (faster modernization conversion, LTSA extensions) and Components (proactive parts pre-ordering from a living BOM registry). It does not capture potential Digital Intelligence revenue from AI platform licensing — a separate value pool.
Range rationale:
5% Bear reflects slow adoption. 25% Bull reflects mature deployment across the EVO-tier 10,000 locomotives, consistent with Wabtec's $210M MBTA modernization win and multibillion-dollar mining order in Q1 2026.
Layer 2: Margin Improvement
Service delivery COGS ($B/yr) Default: $2.1B · Fixed baseline
Source:
Wabtec 2025 Annual Report. Derived from full-year freight services COGS, consistent with an approximately 64% blended COGS ratio on the $3.4B services base.
Calculation:
64% COGS ratio applied to $3.4B freight services base yields ~$2.1B in annual service delivery COGS. This is held fixed across scenarios; the efficiency gain % drives the margin improvement regardless of scenario.
Range rationale:
Held fixed. Efficiency gain % captures the scenario variability — Bear assumes 1.5% reduction in COGS, Base 2.5%, Bull 4.5%.
Efficiency gain (%) Default: 2.5% · Range: 1.0 - 8.0%
Source:
Qualitative analogues from 2025 B2B Services Growth Updated CXO vF.pptx and the Demeter case study referenced above.
Drivers:
Predictive scheduling (reduces yard dwell time in core maintenance), parts pre-staging via BOM registry (eliminates emergency sourcing and Components margin leakage), reduced rework (early obsolescence detection before teardown). These benefits flow primarily through Wabtec's core Services and Components operations, not its Digital segment.
Range rationale:
1% Bear reflects pilot-stage deployment with limited workflow integration. 8% Bull reflects mature, multi-year deployment across the full service network with embedded AI tooling, comparable to industrial OEMs that have deployed at scale.
Layer 3: Customer Savings (Demand Driver)
Fleet size (locomotives) Default: 24,600 · Range: 15,000 - 30,000
Source:
Wabtec corporate materials and investor disclosures on installed base.
Context:
Approximately 24,600 locomotives are currently in the Wabtec installed base. Of these, about 10,000 are EVO-tier units eligible for the Modernization Program and represent the highest-priority opportunity set.
Range rationale:
Bear reflects an EVO-only sub-fleet view (high-priority units only). Bull reflects expansion to international fleets and adjacent rail OEM installed base.
Annual maintenance per unit ($K) Default: $125K · Range: $75 - $200K
Source:
U.S. Department of Energy and California Air Resources Board (CARB) reports on freight line-haul locomotive operating costs.
Context:
Approximately $125K per unit per year is typical for mainline freight locomotives, including parts, labor, and scheduled overhauls.
Range rationale:
$75K Bear reflects switcher and yard locomotives with lighter duty cycles. $200K Bull reflects high-utilization mainline freight units approaching mid-life overhaul.
Maintenance spend reduction (%) Default: 10% · Range: 5 - 20%
Source:
McKinsey reliability and predictive maintenance use case slide in MVI Agent_Wabtec CEO Meeting Prep 032326.pdf.
Benchmark:
10 - 15% maintenance spend reduction is typical from predictive obsolescence management combined with AI-enabled scheduling. The benefit accrues to the customer.
Range rationale:
5% Bear reflects skeptical view limited to parts pre-staging benefits only. 20% Bull reflects full-scale deployment combining predictive scheduling, modernization routing, and unit-level data layer integration.

Calculation Logic

Layer 1 (Revenue uplift) = Freight services base ($3.4B fixed) × Bookings uplift %
Layer 2 (COGS savings) = Service delivery COGS ($2.1B fixed) × Efficiency gain %
Layer 3 (Customer savings) = Fleet size × Annual maintenance per unit × Reduction %
Combined Wabtec value = Layer 1 + Layer 2 (Layer 3 is NOT additive)

Why Layer 3 is separate: Layer 3 represents the customer's economic incentive to buy more service from Wabtec. It is the demand-side proof point that justifies the Layer 1 bookings uplift. Adding Layer 3 to Wabtec's captured value would double-count the same dollar.

What this model does not capture: Digital Intelligence platform or licensing revenue (Wabtec's fastest-growing segment, +75.7% YoY Q1 2026) that could result from commercializing the AI obsolescence layer as a standalone product. This represents a potential additional value pool — a Layer 4 — that would require separate sizing based on platform pricing and addressable fleet beyond Wabtec's own installed base.
